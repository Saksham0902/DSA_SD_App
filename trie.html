Three reflexes from this app

    1

    A tree of prefixes

    Every path from the root spells a prefix. Words that share a beginning share the path, so prefix questions cost O(length of the prefix), no matter how many words you store.

    Spell it out, one edge at a time

    A trie node is just a dict of children (char → node) plus a flag saying “a word ends here”. To insert, walk the characters and create missing children. To search, walk them and fail as soon as a child is missing.

    “cat”, “car” and “cart” all reuse the path c → a. A hash set can tell you if “car” is a word; a trie can also tell you every word that starts with “ca”.

    2

    Searching beyond exact words

    Wildcards, autocomplete, many words on one board, and bits instead of letters.

    When a character could be anything

    A . matches any letter, so at that position you can't follow one child: you must try them all. That's a DFS over the trie that branches at every wildcard and backtracks when a branch dies. Autocomplete is the same DFS without a pattern: walk to the prefix node, then collect every word below it.

    Counting the cost

    Cost depends on the word’s length, not on how many words are stored.

    How to price a trie solution

    1. Insert, search and startsWith cost O(L), where L is the length of the word: one node per character.
    2. That doesn't change with the number of words. Searching a list of N words is O(N·L); a trie is O(L).
    3. Building the trie is the total number of characters inserted.
    4. Space is the number of nodes: at most the total characters, less when prefixes are shared. Each node's children map adds overhead.
