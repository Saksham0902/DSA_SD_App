    Last in, first out

    A stack remembers the most recent unfinished thing. Brackets, expressions, undo, and nested structures all need exactly that.

    The most recent unfinished thing

    When you read ( [ {, the next closer must match {, the most recent opener still waiting. Then [. A stack is exactly that waiting line: push when something opens, pop when it closes. If the top doesn't match, the string is broken.

    2

    The monotonic stack

    “Next greater element” for every position in one pass. The stack holds everyone still waiting for their answer.

    A line of people waiting for a warmer day

    Daily Temperatures: for each day, how long until a warmer one? Brute force looks ahead from every day: O(n²). Flip it around. Walk forward and keep a stack of days that haven't found their warmer day yet. When today is warmer than the day on top, today is that day's answer. Pop it and keep checking.

    The days left in the stack are always in decreasing temperature order, which is why it's called monotonic.

    Counting the cost

    Each element is pushed once and popped once, however deep the while loop looks.

    How to price a stack solution

    1. Count pushes and pops, not loop nesting. In a monotonic stack every element is pushed exactly once and popped at most once.
    2. Total operations ≤ 2n → O(n), even though a while sits inside a for.
    3. Space is the stack's maximum height: O(n) in the worst case (e.g. a strictly decreasing input never pops).
